What if you never told an agent where the goal was, what a wall meant, or what "good" looked like — just handed it a point score after every move?

I built a demo that drops a Q-learning agent into a grid maze with a hidden goal and a hazard to avoid, and lets it figure out the entire route from scratch, purely from trial, error, and a running scoreboard.

The scoreboard IS the teacher: every step costs -1, wandering into the hazard costs -20 and ends the run, reaching the goal pays +50. No hints, no directions — the agent starts by bumping into walls and hazards essentially at random.

The table that remembers: underneath, it's keeping one number per (cell, direction) pair — a "Q-value" estimating how good that move is long-term. After every step it nudges that estimate toward the reward it just got, plus a discounted guess of how good things look from where it landed. Do that thousands of times and the good estimates near the goal quietly propagate backward through the whole grid.

Explore, then exploit: early on the agent ignores its own table and just wanders (high "epsilon"). As epsilon decays, it leans more on what it's learned. Watch the heatmap — dark means "still unknown," gold means "the agent is confident this is a great place to be" — and the little arrows snap into a clean route as training goes on.

This loop (try something, get a score, update a belief, repeat) is the same backbone behind game-playing AI and a lot of modern reinforcement learning — the "smarts" is really just very disciplined bookkeeping over experience. Full training run in the video below.
