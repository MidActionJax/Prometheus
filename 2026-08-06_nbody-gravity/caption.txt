🪐 What happens when you give a handful of objects nothing but gravity and let them go?

I built a simulation of true N-body gravity — every object pulling on every other object at once, no fixed sun, no scripted orbits, no shortcuts.

The physics: every frame, each body sums up Newton's law of gravitation (F = G·m1·m2/r²) pulling it toward every other body in the system, then updates its velocity and position from that combined force. Nothing about the motion is hand-drawn — it's entirely a byproduct of that math running 60 times a second.

Why it looks chaotic: with only two bodies, gravity has a clean, solvable answer — a perfect ellipse. Add a third body and you get the actual "three-body problem," a system with no general closed-form solution, where tiny differences in starting position snowball into completely different paths. That's deterministic chaos: identical rules every time, but no two runs ever look alike.

Click anywhere in the demo to fling a new body into the system and watch it get folded into (or flung back out of) the gravitational dance in real time.

This is the same underlying math behind everything from planetary motion to galaxy formation, just small enough to watch unfold on a screen. Video below.
